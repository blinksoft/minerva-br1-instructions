# S5 — Stability margin and the swing test

## Slot
Step 10 motor mount cure, second half. Fallback: install cure.

## Objective
The cadet can state the stability rule (CG ahead of CP by one to two body diameters), say what happens
when it is broken in either direction, and run a swing test.

## Live on the day
Play the video. When the pause card comes up, pause it, take two or three answers from the room, then
press play. The video gives the answer. That is the whole job; no props, no setup.

## Script outline
1. **Put the two dots together.** CG from S3, CP from S4. A rocket flies straight when the CG is in front
   of the CP. Air pushes at the CP, the rocket pivots around the CG, so a push behind the pivot swings the
   nose back into the wind. Like a weathervane. Like the feathers on an arrow.
2. **How far in front.** Measure the distance from CG to CP and divide by the body tube diameter. That
   number is the stability margin in calibers. One to two calibers is the target. Under one is risky. Over
   two or three is a different kind of problem.
   - **Pause card.** On screen: "This rocket has its CG one diameter ahead of its CP. I load a heavier motor. More stable or less?" Hold five seconds with a countdown, then a chime. On resume: Less. The motor is mass at the back, so the CG slides aft toward the CP and the margin shrinks. That is why the loaded rocket is what you check.
3. **Too little: it tumbles.** CG behind the CP and any wobble grows instead of shrinking. The rocket
   cartwheels off the pad. Demo: the paper airplane with no paperclip.
4. **Too much: it weathercocks.** A very stable rocket turns hard into any crosswind and flies sideways
   instead of up. Lower apogee, long walk to recover it. Demo: the paper airplane with two paperclips on
   the nose, thrown in front of a box fan.
5. **The swing test.** Tie a string at the CG, with the motor loaded, and swing the rocket in a circle
   overhead. Nose forward and steady means stable. If it flies sideways or backwards, do not launch it. Do
   this with the finished BR-1 outside the classroom before the first flight.

## Demo, filmed
- Two paper airplanes, one plain, one with paperclips on the nose.
- A box fan for the crosswind.
- A finished BR-1 with a spent motor casing loaded, string tied at the CG, for the swing test (outdoors or
  a high-ceilinged room).
- Optional: a rocket on a pivot (a skewer through the CG into a stand) in front of the box fan as a
  stand-in for a wind tunnel. Shift the pivot forward and back to show it swing straight or flip.

## Pause point
After beat 2. The card reads: "This rocket has its CG one diameter ahead of its CP. I load a heavier motor. More stable or less?"

On resume the video answers: Less. The motor is mass at the back, so the CG slides aft toward the CP and the margin shrinks. That is why the loaded rocket is what you check.

## Shot list
- Bench camera: two dots on the BR-1 (tape marks for CG and CP), a ruler between them, the caliber number
  said aloud.
- Paper airplanes thrown toward camera, with and without the paperclips.
- Pivot rocket in front of the fan: stable, then pivot moved aft, then flip.
- Wide shot outdoors: the swing test, nose leading.

- Pause card and answer card as full-screen title cards, see production.md.

## Check before filming
- Nothing regulatory. Confirm the pivot demo works before recording; it needs a light rocket and a fan
  strong enough to matter.
