# S4 — Center of pressure

## Slot
Step 13 motor mount cure, first half. Fallback: install cure.

## Objective
The cadet can explain what the center of pressure is, which parts of the rocket make it, and why fins
at the back move it aft.

## Live on the day
Play the video. When the pause card comes up, pause it, take two or three answers from the room, then
press play. The video gives the answer. That is the whole job; no props, no setup.

## Script outline
1. **The air pushes too.** When a rocket flies at a slight angle, air pushes sideways on everything: nose,
   tube, fins. All those pushes add up to one push at one point. That point is the center of pressure, CP.
2. **Where it is depends on shape, not weight.** A big fin catches a lot of air, so the CP sits near the
   fins. A rocket with tiny fins has its CP further forward. Nothing about the CP changes when you add a
   motor or nose weight; those move the CG, not the CP.
3. **Where the push comes from.** Not every part pushes equally. At the small angles a rocket really flies
   at, the nose cone makes a little sideways force, a smooth body tube makes almost none, and the fins make
   most of it. That is why the BR-1's CP sits near the fins, not in the middle of the tube. (The old
   cardboard-cutout demo was dropped on 2026-09-21: it balances the side view's area, which counts the
   tube equally, and on the BR-1 it lands about 7 1/2 in ahead of the real CP, ahead of the loaded CG.)
   - **Pause card.** On screen: "If I took the fins off this rocket, which way would the CP move?" Hold five seconds with a countdown, then a chime. On resume: Forward. The fins are most of the push and they are at the back; take them away and what is left adds up near the nose.
4. **What the software does.** It uses the Barrowman equations, a set
   of formulas from 1966 that compute how much sideways force each part produces and where, then adds
   them up. You will see the CP as a red dot on the rocket in S10.
5. **The trick.** Fins are at the back for one reason: to drag the CP aft. Next segment: why aft is where
   we want it.
