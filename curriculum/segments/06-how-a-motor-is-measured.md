# S6 — How a motor is measured

## Slot
Step 17 wait (back of the rings and eyebolt nuts curing), 5 to 10 minutes. Fallback: step 26 wait.

## Objective
The cadet can say what a Newton-second is in plain words and read the letter ladder from A to O.

## Live on the day
Play the video. When the pause card comes up, pause it, take two or three answers from the room, then
press play. The video gives the answer. That is the whole job; no props, no setup.
Optional: hold up the printed ladder during the pause.

## Script outline
1. **A Newton is a push.** Hold an apple. The Earth pulls on it with about one Newton. A rocket motor
   pushes with tens or hundreds of Newtons.
2. **Push for how long.** A shove that lasts a tenth of a second and a lean that lasts ten seconds can
   move a cart the same distance. Multiply push by time and you get total impulse, in Newton-seconds.
   That is the single number that says how much a motor can do.
3. **The letter ladder.** Motors are sorted by total impulse into letters. Each letter is double the one
   before. A is up to 2.5 Newton-seconds. B 5, C 10, D 20, E 40, F 80, G 160. Then high power: H 320, I 640,
   J 1280, K 2560, L 5120, M 10240, N 20480, O 40960. Point at the printed ladder while saying it.
   - **Pause card.** On screen: "An F motor and a G motor: how many times more total push does the G have, at most?" Hold five seconds with a countdown, then a chime. On resume: Two, at the top of each range. Each letter doubles. An A next to a G is six doublings, sixty-four times.
4. **Feel the gap.** The Alpha III's A8 has about 2.5 Newton-seconds. The BR-1's G74 has up to 160. That
   is six doublings, sixty-four times the total push. The H you might certify on is double that again.
5. **The letter is the size of the box, not the whole story.** Two G motors can push very differently:
   one hard and short, one gentle and long. The next segment reads the rest of the code.

## Pause point
After beat 3. The card reads: "An F motor and a G motor: how many times more total push does the G have, at most?"

On resume the video answers: Two, at the top of each range. Each letter doubles. An A next to a G is six doublings, sixty-four times.
