# S2 — NAR, Tripoli and certification

## Slot

## Objective
The cadet can name the two national organizations, say what a Level 1 certification is, and know when
they are old enough to try for one.

## Live on the day
Play the video. When the pause card comes up, pause it, take two or three answers from the room, then
press play. The video gives the answer. That is the whole job; no props, no setup.

## Script outline
1. **Two clubs, one rulebook.** The National Association of Rocketry (NAR) and the Tripoli Rocketry
   Association (TRA). Both write safety codes, insure launches, certify flyers and certify motors. A
   launch you attend is run by a section or prefecture of one of them, sometimes both.
2. **Why the field has a boss.** The Range Safety Officer (RSO) inspects every rocket before it flies and
   can say no. The Launch Control Officer (LCO) runs the pads and the countdown. High power launches fly
   under an FAA waiver that opens a column of airspace for the day. Nobody freelances.
   - **Pause card.** On screen: "What does the RSO look for on a rocket before letting it fly?" Hold five seconds with a countdown, then a chime. On resume: Stability with the motor loaded, motor held in, recovery harness anchored, chute packed and protected, no igniter installed, a certified motor matched to the rocket, and a build that will take the flight.
3. **The certification ladder.** Level 1: H and I motors. Fly a rocket on one, recover it in flyable
   condition, get it signed off. Level 2: J, K and L, plus a written exam on the rules and the physics.
   Level 3: M, N and O, built under the eye of a committee with documentation. Each level proves you can
   handle the next size up safely.
4. **How old you have to be.** NAR Junior Level 1 lets 14 to 17 year olds certify with an adult member
   supervising. Tripoli's Mentoring Program lets junior members fly high power with a certified mentor from
   age 12. Adults certify at 18. Under 14 in our program, you fly the BR-1 on a G and learn the rest.
5. **What this rocket is for.** First flight on a G74 for those not certifying. First flight on an H for
   Junior L1 and adult L1 candidates, on this same airframe, as the certification attempt. Certification
   is about the flyer, not the rocket, and this rocket is enough.
