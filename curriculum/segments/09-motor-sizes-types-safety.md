# S9 — Motor sizes, types and safety

## Slot
Second fin wait (step 29), 5 minutes. Fallback: step 26 wait if S7 was done before lunch.

## Objective
The cadet can name the common motor diameters, tell a black powder motor from a composite one, and repeat
the program's motor handling rules.

## Live on the day
Play the video. When the pause card comes up, pause it, take two or three answers from the room, then
press play. The video gives the answer. That is the whole job; no props, no setup.
Optional: hold up one spent 29 mm casing during the pause.

## Script outline
1. **Diameter is the fit.** Motors come in standard diameters: 18 mm for the Alpha III, then 24, 29, 38,
   54, 75 and 98. The BR-1's motor mount is 29 mm. Every 29 mm motor from a G to a small H fits it, which
   is why one airframe covers both the everyday flight and the certification flight.
2. **Two kinds of propellant.** Black powder motors are the small paper-cased ones: cheap, simple, up to
   about D or E. Composite motors use ammonium perchlorate composite propellant, APCP, the same family as
   the Space Shuttle boosters. Much more energy per gram, plastic or fiberglass cases, from D all the way
   to O.
3. **Single use or reload.** A single-use motor is thrown away after the flight. A reloadable motor is a
   metal casing you own, plus a reload kit of propellant grains, liner, nozzle and delay you assemble
   before each flight. Reloads are cheaper per flight once you own the case, and assembling one is a skill
   in itself.
4. **The rules on this bench.** Motors stay in the range box until launch day. No motor, igniter or black
   powder on a build table, ever. Motors are stored cool, dry and in their packaging.
   - **Pause card.** On screen: "On the high power pads the igniter goes in last, at the pad. At our low power table the RSO wants the igniter and plug in before you walk up. Why the difference?" Hold five seconds with a countdown, then a chime. On resume: An installed igniter is the only thing that can start the motor. On a G or H there is enough energy that the range takes no chances, and you are leaning over the rail to install it. Low power igniters are small and the pads are a shared rack that has to turn over fast, so the RSO checks the rocket at the table and takes the trade. Either way, the rule is the same: do what your RSO says.
5. **Why.** A motor cannot tell the difference between a launch pad and a classroom. Every rule here exists
   because someone learned it the hard way.
