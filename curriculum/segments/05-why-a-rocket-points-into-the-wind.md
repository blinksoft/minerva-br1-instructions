# S5 — Why a rocket points into the wind

## Slot
Step 13 motor mount cure, second of three, between Center of pressure and Stability margin. Fallback: install cure.

## Objective
The cadet can explain why a rocket with its CP behind its CG straightens itself after a gust (the air
pushes at the CP, the rocket pivots at the CG, a push behind the pivot makes a restoring twist), and
what happens when the CP is in front.

## Live on the day
Play the video. When the pause card comes up, pause it, take two or three answers from the room, then
press play. The video gives the answer. That is the whole job; no props, no setup.

## Script outline
1. **Start with a weather vane.** It turns on a rod through its balance point, the CG. Its big flat tail
   catches the air, so the CP sits back in the tail. Pivot in the middle, push at the back.
2. **A push behind the pivot.** The wind shifts. The air pushes at the CP in the wind's direction; the rod
   pushes back at the CG. Two equal and opposite pushes that are no longer on one line make a twist (a
   moment, or torque). The twist swings the tail downwind and the nose upwind until the pushes line up.
   A push behind a pivot always points the thing into the wind.
3. **Three pushes on a rocket.** Weight at the CG, drag at the CP (the air rushing past a climbing
   rocket), thrust from the tail. Flying straight, all three sit on one line and nothing twists.
   - **Pause card.** On screen: "A gust hits the rocket from the side. Which point does it turn around?" Hold five seconds with a countdown, then a chime. On resume: The CG. Anything moving freely through the air spins about its balance point; the vane's rod sat at its CG for the same reason. The CG is the pivot, the CP is the push.
4. **CP behind: the gust fixes itself.** The BR-1 with fins on. A gust tips the nose; the drag at the CP
   grows and swings sideways; that push is behind the pivot, so the twist turns the nose back into the
   airflow. It straightens on its own. A weather vane with a motor.
5. **CP in front: the gust wins.** Fins off, so the CP lands ahead of the CG (the Center of pressure
   segment's pause answer). Same gust, same tip, but the push is in front of the pivot: the twist tips the
   nose further, the rocket comes around, and the thrust drives it toward the ground.
6. **Behind, and by how much.** CP behind the CG: every gust straightens the rocket. CP in front: every
   gust grows. That is why fins are at the back of every rocket on the table. How far behind is enough is
   the Stability margin segment.

## Pause point
After beat 3. The card reads: "A gust hits the rocket from the side. Which point does it turn around?"

On resume the video answers: The CG. Anything moving freely through the air spins about its balance
point. The CG is the pivot, the CP is the push, and whether the push is behind the pivot or in front of it
decides what the twist does.
