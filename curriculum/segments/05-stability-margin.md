# S5 — Stability margin

## Slot
Step 10 motor mount cure, second half. Fallback: install cure.

## Objective
The cadet can state the stability rule (CG ahead of CP by one to three body diameters), say what happens
when it is broken in either direction, and know how the program checks it before a flight.

## Live on the day
Play the video. When the pause card comes up, pause it, take two or three answers from the room, then
press play. The video gives the answer. That is the whole job; no props, no setup.

## Script outline
1. **Put the two dots together.** CG from S3, CP from S4. A rocket flies straight when the CG is in front
   of the CP. Air pushes at the CP, the rocket pivots around the CG, so a push behind the pivot swings the
   nose back into the wind. Like a weathervane. Like the feathers on an arrow.
2. **How far in front.** Measure the distance from CG to CP and divide by the body tube diameter. That
   number is the stability margin in calibers. One to three calibers is the target. Under one is risky.
   Well over three is a different kind of problem.
   - **Pause card.** On screen: "This rocket has its CG one diameter ahead of its CP. I load a heavier motor. More stable or less?" Hold five seconds with a countdown, then a chime. On resume: Less. The motor is mass at the back, so the CG slides aft toward the CP and the margin shrinks. That is why the loaded rocket is what you check.
3. **Too little: it tumbles.** CG behind the CP and any wobble grows instead of shrinking. The rocket
   cartwheels off the pad.
4. **Too much: it weathercocks.** A very stable rocket turns hard into any crosswind and flies sideways
   instead of up. Lower apogee, long walk to recover it.
5. **How the program checks it.** OpenRocket puts both dots on the BR-1 and reads the margin in calibers with
   the chosen motor (S12). The builder's part is the finger balance from S3: motor loaded, compare the balance
   point with the sim's CG. No swing test: it has damaged too many rockets.
