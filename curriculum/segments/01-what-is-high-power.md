# S1 — What is high power rocketry?

## Slot
Alpha III glue wait, around 8:45. Fallback: right after the safety briefing at 9:30, before rail buttons.

## Objective
The cadet can say what makes a rocket "high power" instead of a model rocket, and point to which of the
two rockets on the table is which.

## Live on the day
Play the video. When the pause card comes up, pause it, take two or three answers from the room, then
press play. The video gives the answer. That is the whole job; no props, no setup.
Optional: hold up the A8, G and H casings during the pause.

## Script outline
1. **Two rockets on the table.** Hold up the Alpha III they are gluing and a finished BR-1. Same idea:
   tube, nose, fins, motor, parachute. One is a model rocket, one is high power. What is the difference?
   It is not the size of the airframe. It is the motor and the weight.
2. **The four thresholds.** A rocket is high power if any one of these is true: the motors together have
   more than 160 Newton-seconds of total impulse (anything above a G), any motor averages more than 80
   Newtons of thrust, the propellant weighs more than 125 grams, or the rocket weighs more than 1500 grams
   at liftoff. Put the numbers on a card; do not expect anyone to remember them yet.
   - **Pause card.** On screen: "Which of these three motors makes a rocket high power?" Hold five seconds with a countdown, then a chime. On resume: Only the H. A G is the biggest motor you can fly and still be a model rocket; the H crosses the 160 Newton-second line.
3. **Why the line matters.** Above the line the FAA cares. The launch needs a waiver, the field needs to
   be bigger, the flyer needs to be certified, and the rocket has to be built to take it. That is why the
   BR-1 has through-the-wall fins and a motor retainer, and the Alpha III has a glued-on fin and a clip.
4. **Where the BR-1 sits.** On a G74 it is a mid-power rocket that anyone can fly under the model rocket
   safety code. Put an H in the same mount and it is high power, which is why it works as a Level 1
   certification rocket. Same build, two worlds.
5. **What today is.** You are all building the same rocket. On its first flight, some of you will fly it
   on a G. Those going for a certification will fly it on an H, first flight, as their Level 1 attempt.
   Same airframe, so build it like it is going to carry an H, because it might be yours that does.

## Pause point
After beat 2. The card reads: "Which of these three motors makes a rocket high power?"

On resume the video answers: Only the H. A G is the biggest motor you can fly and still be a model rocket; the H crosses the 160 Newton-second line.
