# S02 — NAR, Tripoli and certification: narration script

Record one file per numbered cue, as `voice/NN.wav` (any sample rate, mono is fine).
Leave a beat of silence at the start and end of each. Re-run render.py and the slides
re-time themselves to your voice.

**01** `voice/01.wav`

Segment two. NAR, Tripoli and certification. By the end of this you will be able to name the two national rocketry organizations, say what a Level 1 certification is, and know when you are old enough to try for one.

**02** `voice/02.wav`

Start with who is in charge. There are two national rocketry organizations. The National Association of Rocketry, NAR. And the Tripoli Rocketry Association, Tripoli. Both do the same four jobs. They write the safety code, the rulebook every launch follows. They insure launches. They certify flyers, which means they test you and sign you off. And they certify motors. Segment seven shows what that means on the label. A launch is run by a local chapter of one of them. NAR calls it a section, Tripoli a prefecture. This program flies under NAR. Georgia has no Tripoli prefecture right now.

**03** `voice/03.wav`

A launch field has a boss. The Range Safety Officer, the RSO, looks at every rocket before it flies. Yours, and the instructor's. The RSO can say no, and that is the end of it. The Launch Control Officer, the LCO, runs the pads and the countdown. Nobody presses the button except the LCO. And the air above the field is not free. A high power launch flies under an FAA waiver. The Federal Aviation Administration opens a column of airspace over the field for that day. Nobody freelances. Do what your RSO says.

**04 — pause card, no narration.** The card reads: _What does the RSO look for on a rocket before letting it fly?_

**05** `voice/05.wav`

Seven things. Stability with the motor loaded, so it flies straight. The motor held in, so it cannot shoot out the back. The recovery harness anchored. The chute packed and protected from the ejection blast. No igniter installed. That goes in at the pad, last. A certified motor matched to the rocket. And a build that will take the flight. Now look at your bench. The threaded plastic part on the aft end of the motor tube is the motor retainer. That is item two on the RSO's list, and you built it today.

**06** `voice/06.wav`

Motors come in letters, A up to O, and Segment six climbs that ladder. Up to G is a model rocket motor. H and above is high power, and that takes a certification. Level 1 is H and I. Fly a rocket on one, get it back in flyable condition, get it signed off. Level 2 is J, K and L. The same flight, plus a written exam on the rules and the physics. Level 3 is M, N and O. Built under the eye of a committee, with documentation. Each level proves you can handle the next size up.

**07** `voice/07.wav`

So when can you try? NAR has Junior Level 1. From fourteen to seventeen you can certify, with an adult member supervising you. Tripoli has a Mentoring Program. From twelve, a junior member can fly high power with a certified mentor. At eighteen you certify as an adult. In this program, if you are under fourteen, you fly the BR-1 on a G and learn everything else. When you turn fourteen, you already know the rocket.

**08** `voice/08.wav`

That is what this rocket is for. Segment one drew the line: a G keeps it a model rocket, an H makes it high power. So a Level 1 attempt, Junior or adult, is this same airframe on an H, an H115 or an H135 for example, whichever the program can get that season. That first flight is the test. Certification is about the flyer, not the rocket. And this rocket is enough.

**09** `voice/09.wav`

The one thing to remember. Two clubs, one rulebook. And Level 1 is an H or an I motor, flown and recovered in one piece, on the rocket you are building right now.
