Before you start

Read the whole page once before you open the epoxy. Several steps have a cure wait built in, and knowing what is coming keeps you from gluing yourself into a corner.

Work on a covered bench. Epoxy does not come off finished furniture.

Dry-fit every part before it gets glue on it. Every step that says "test fit" is there because parts vary.

Your body tube is white. Some photos in this guide show an older kraft-brown tube — the parts and steps are the same.

Safety

!
Epoxy is a skin sensitizer. Every exposure raises the odds of a permanent allergic reaction, so wear nitrile gloves whenever you mix, spread or clean up epoxy. Never wipe it off skin with solvent; wash with soap and water.
!
Wear eye protection when sanding, when mixing epoxy, and whenever you use superglue. Superglue can spatter, and it bonds to the eye instantly.
!
Work in a ventilated area. Epoxy and superglue fumes build up fast in a closed room.
!
Superglue bonds skin instantly. Keep acetone or debonder nearby, and never put your finger behind a hole you are about to glue into unless the step tells you to.
!
No motors, igniters, or black powder anywhere near the build table.

01Rail Buttons

These go on the body tube, not the motor mount. Do them first — once the motor mount is glued inside, you cannot reach the forward hole.

1

Install the forward rail button

The forward hole is too far down the tube to reach with a finger, so the weld nut gets pulled into place with a piece of 18–22 gauge wire about 24 inches long.

  1. Thread the wire in through the forward rail button hole from the outside and out the aft end of the tube.
  2. Slide the weld nut onto the wire, pointy side toward the inside of the tube. Tie an overhand knot (shoe knot) in the wire so the nut cannot come off.
  3. Put superglue around the weld nut, then gently pull the wire until the nut seats in the hole and the point is sticking out. Hold for 60 seconds while the glue sets.
  4. Cut the wire off right at the nut with scissors or wire cutters.
  5. Put the rail button on the nut — flat side toward the tube, concave side out — and start the screw by hand.
  6. Pull the rail button outward while you screw it down. Snug is good.
i
The numbered photos below match the numbered steps above — arrows and labels are from the original build briefing.
i
If you have never done this, do a test pull with no glue first.
!
Be very careful not to push the nut back into the body — you cannot get it back.
!
Do not overtighten. The rail button will crack.
2

Install the aft rail button

The aft hole is close enough to the end that you can hold the nut with a finger, so no wire needed.

  1. Balance the weld nut on your fingertip and put superglue around its inner edge.
  2. Reach in through the aft end and press the nut into the hole from the inside.
  3. Keep your finger on the back of the nut and put the rail button on — flat side toward the tube, concave side out. Start the screw by hand, then pull the button outward while you tighten it.
i
The numbered photos below match the numbered steps above — arrows and labels are from the original build briefing.
i
Practice putting the nut into the hole dry before you add glue.
!
Snug only. Do not crack the button.

02Motor Mount

Build the motor mount as its own subassembly. It has to be square and fully cured before it goes anywhere near the body tube.

3

Sand the motor mount tube

The cut ends of the motor mount tube usually have some fuzzy excess cardboard. Sand those smooth.

Sand the whole outside of the tube to knock off the shiny coating.

i
That glossy coating is what epoxy fails to stick to. Dull the entire surface the centering rings will sit on.
4

Test fit the centering rings

Slide each centering ring onto the motor mount tube. They should go on, but they should not be loose.

If a ring will not start, sand the inside diameter a little at a time and try again.

!
A tight fit is what you want. Sand the ring, not the tube — you already sized the tube in step 3.
5

Mark the aft end

Set the threaded end of the motor retainer onto the aft end of the motor mount tube.

Slide the aft centering ring down until it touches the retainer.

Mark all the way around the tube at the forward edge of the ring.

i
That line is the forward edge of your epoxy zone for step 8. Everything aft of it gets glue.
6

Mark the forward end

Make a mark ¼ inch down from the forward (top) end of the motor mount tube.

Slide the forward centering ring — the one with the eyebolt hole — down to that mark.

Mark all the way around the tube at both edges of the ring.

7

Move the rings clear

Take the forward centering ring back off the tube.

Slide the aft centering ring to about the middle of the tube so it is out of the way.

8

Epoxy the motor retainer and aft centering ring

⏱
5-minute epoxy

Spread epoxy on the tube from the aft end up to the mark you made in step 5.

Slide the motor retainer on, then slide the aft centering ring down until it touches the retainer.

Check that the ring is square to the tube before the epoxy grabs.

!
Do not get any epoxy inside the motor mount tube. If you do, wipe it out immediately with alcohol — a cured ridge inside the tube will keep the motor from seating.
!
Keep epoxy off the retainer threads.
9

Epoxy the forward centering ring

⏱
5-minute epoxy

Run a band of epoxy all the way around the tube between the two forward marks.

Slide the forward centering ring down onto that band.

!
Get the ring perpendicular to the tube and hold it there. A cocked ring means the motor mount will not go into the body tube straight, and the whole rocket flies crooked.
10

Let the motor mount cure

Stand the assembly up with the retainer end pointing up and leave it alone until the epoxy is hard.

i
Standing it retainer-up lets gravity keep the rings seated instead of sagging.

03Eyebolt & Recovery Anchor

The eyebolt in the forward centering ring is what the whole recovery system hangs off. Do not skimp here.

11

Assemble the eyebolt

Put a nut and a washer onto the eyebolt.

Pass the eyebolt through the hole in the forward centering ring from the forward side.

Add the second washer and nut on the back and tighten with the two 7/16 wrenches.

12

Epoxy the back side of the centering rings

⏱
5-minute epoxy

Fillet epoxy into the joint between each centering ring and the motor mount tube, on the side you have not glued yet.

Put epoxy over both eyebolt nuts and washers. Not a lot — just enough that they can never back off.

Stand the assembly motor-mount-down to cure.

13

Test fit the motor mount in the body tube

Slide the motor mount into the body tube. Go in at a slight angle to clear the aft rail button nut.

Sand the outside diameter of the centering rings as needed. You want snug — it should slide with some resistance, not drop in.

!
Do this dry, and get the fit right now. Once there is 15-minute epoxy inside the body tube you have one shot.
14

Tie the Kevlar cord to the eyebolt

Tie the 5 ft Kevlar cord to the eyebolt with a bowline.

A bowline gives you a fixed loop that will not slip and will not jam shut under shock load, which is exactly what you want on the recovery anchor.

i
Rabbit out of the hole, around the tree, back down the hole. Then dress it and pull it tight before you trust it.
!
Pull the correct side tight. The last frame of the diagram shows which end to load — pull the wrong one and the knot capsizes instead of setting.

Animated step-by-step bowline
15

Protect the Kevlar from epoxy

Tape over the cord where it meets the eyebolt with masking or painter's tape.

Feed the loose end of the cord down through the motor mount tube so it is out of the way while you glue.

!
Epoxy soaked into the Kevlar makes it stiff and brittle right where it takes the most shock. Tape it.

04Install the Motor Mount

15-minute epoxy from here — you need the working time. Read all four steps before you mix.

16

Mark your epoxy stick

Hold a stir stick against the motor mount and mark the distance between the two centering rings on it.

That mark tells you how far down the body tube to reach when you put the glue ring inside.

i
The sticks in the current kits are long enough that you do not need to tape an extension on.
17

Put a ring of epoxy inside the body tube

⏱
15-minute epoxy

Using the marked stick as your depth guide, lay a ring of epoxy around the inside of the body tube where the forward centering ring will land.

18

Start the motor mount in and add epoxy

⏱
15-minute epoxy

Partially insert the motor mount.

Spread more epoxy at the aft end of the body tube where the aft centering ring will sit.

Put a little epoxy on the rail button backing nut and the tube around it.

19

Seat the motor mount

Push the motor mount the rest of the way in until it is fully seated as shown.

Let the epoxy cure completely before you go on.

!
Do not move to the fins until this is fully hard. The fins locate off the motor mount.
20

Epoxy the aft joint

⏱
5-minute epoxy

Fillet epoxy into the joint between the aft centering ring and the body tube, all the way around.

!
Keep epoxy off the retainer threads. Easiest way: thread the retainer screw cap on first, glue, then take it back off.

05Fins

Three fins, through-the-wall into the motor mount. Alignment here is what makes the rocket fly straight.

21

Test fit the fins

Slide each fin into its slot dry. The fin root should reach the motor mount tube and the fin should stand straight out along a radius — not leaning.

Wood thickness varies, so you may need to sand the slots to get a fin to go in.

!
Sand the slot, never the fin. Thinning the fin weakens it and opens up a gap you cannot fill.
22

Epoxy the fins

⏱
5-minute epoxy

Put epoxy along the root (back) edge of the fin and slide it into the slot.

Make sure the root contacts the motor mount tube and the fin is aligned before the epoxy sets.

Do one fin at a time.

23

Fillet the fin roots

⏱
5-minute epoxy

Every fin gets an epoxy fillet on both sides where it meets the body tube — six fillets in all. You do them two at a time, letting gravity hold the epoxy in the joint.

  1. Lay the rocket on the table resting on one fin, so the other two fins point up in a V. Roll it until both raised fins are at the same angle — the joints between those fins and the tube should face straight up.
  2. Mix a small batch of plain 5-minute epoxy. Do not thicken it or add anything to it.
  3. Dab the epoxy into the joint along the root of each raised fin, on the side facing up. Work from the front of the fin to the back so the whole root is covered. It will settle into a smooth fillet on its own.
  4. Wait 5 minutes for it to set.
  5. Roll the rocket onto the next fin so a fresh pair of joints faces up, and repeat.
  6. Do it one more time. After three rounds every fin root is epoxied to the body on both sides.
i
Small dabs, not a bead. Plain 5-minute epoxy is thin enough to flow into the joint by itself; a big bead runs down the tube.
!
Do not move the rocket until the epoxy has set. If it rolls while wet the fillet slumps to one side.

06Recovery Harness

Kevlar to nylon, nylon to nose cone, and a loop in the middle for the parachute.

24

Tie the nylon shock cord to the Kevlar

Join the 10 ft nylon cord to the Kevlar with a double sheet bend. A sheet bend is the right knot for joining two lines of different diameter, and doubling it keeps it from working loose in slick nylon.

Make the bight in the thicker line (the nylon) and pass the thinner line (the Kevlar) through it.

i
In the photo the blue rope is the nylon (bigger) and the yellow rope is the Kevlar (smaller).
!
Pull the thick end and the thin ends in the directions the last frame shows. Loaded the wrong way the bend slips, and slick nylon is unforgiving about it.

25

Tie the nylon to the nose cone

Tie the free end of the nylon shock cord around the bar inside the nose cone shoulder with a bowline.

!
Pull the correct side tight. The last frame of the diagram shows which end to load — pull the wrong one and the knot capsizes instead of setting.
↗

26

Make the parachute attachment point

Tie an overhand knot about 12 inches down from the nose cone attachment point.

Fold the shock cord in half at that spot and use the looped end to tie around the rest of the cord, making a fixed loop.

This is where the parachute clips in at the field.

i
Parachutes are loaned out on launch day rather than included in the kit — they run about $15 each, and keeping them program property keeps the participant fee down. Hand yours back after recovery.

07Finish & Homework

27

Decorate your rocket

Paint, temporary tattoos, and other decoration are all fair game. Prizes may be given for the best-designed rocket.

Skip stickers and tape — at flight speed they peel off and change how the rocket flies.

28

Homework: simulate your rocket in OpenRocket

Learn to simulate your build. Play with launch angle and wind to see how far you might have to walk, and improve your odds of getting the rocket back.

Start from the program's own BR-1 model rather than building the airframe from scratch — open it in OpenRocket, then change motors, mass and conditions and watch what happens.
