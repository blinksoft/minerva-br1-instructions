## Title

Why a rocket points into the wind — BR-1 Build Day, Segment 5

## Description

Why a rocket with its CP behind its CG straightens itself after a gust, and why one with the CP in front comes around and dives. A weather vane, then the three pushes on a rocket.

Segment 5 of the "While the epoxy cures" series for the Georgia Wing High Power Rocketry Minerva BR-1 build day (Civil Air Patrol). Each segment is 3 to 5 minutes and fills an epoxy cure wait during the build.

Pause card: when the countdown appears, instructors pause, ask the room, then press play for the answer. This segment asks: A gust hits the rocket from the side. Which point does it turn around?

Assembly guide: https://blinksoft.github.io/minerva-br1-instructions/
Curriculum and scripts: https://github.com/blinksoft/minerva-br1-instructions/tree/main/curriculum

Narration is synthesized (ElevenLabs) from the program's script. Slides are generated from the curriculum source in the guide's repository.

Sequence after "Rocket Stability" by Angelo State University Engineering (public domain), youtu.be/pnY5hkmJS58; redrawn for the BR-1
Weather vane: Rooster Weathervane, 19th century, sheet and wrought iron, Smithsonian American Art Museum 1986.65.366 (CC0, Smithsonian Open Access)
BR-1 geometry, CG and CP: the program's OpenRocket file (files/BR-1.ork), designed by Michael Bright
